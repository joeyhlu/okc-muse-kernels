## What limits the WG path (box evidence)

The idea: wgmma m64nNk16 RS. A is the dequantized weight fragments in registers, i.e. v2's (ag, a8) wiring, which is
the per-warp SM90 RS A layout. B is the staged X slice in shared memory, read through a no-swizzle K-major
descriptor. One B read per warpgroup per k16 step, instead of mma.sync's per-warp re-reads. That allows 32/64 tokens
per pass, so a hot expert is streamed and dequantized 2–4× less often.

1. **ptxas serializes wgmma in every instance at ≤ 80 regs.**
   - Every instance at ≤ 80 regs reports `C7512 Potential Performance Loss: wgmma.mma_async instructions are
     serialized due to insufficient register resources`. That is all 64- and 80-register variants.
   - The two-half A double buffer with `wait_group 1` therefore buys nothing.
   - The same tiling with and without serialization: on bs128 skewed, WG64/U4 at 2 CTAs/SM (128 regs, not
     serialized) runs 231.6 µs; at 3 CTAs/SM (80 regs, serialized) it runs 290.7 µs.
   - The mma.sync path is already at 64 regs with 120 B of spills, so the wgmma accumulators and A registers do not
     fit next to the weight loads in flight.
2. **The per-unit cost is higher than mma.sync's even at the same pass count.**
   - WG16 vs mma16 (same tokens per pass, same units): +13% at bs 128/256 and +22–35% at bs 8–32, where every
     expert is a single cold pass.
   - That is structural, not a pass effect:
     - serialized async MMAs;
     - `.sync.aligned` lockstep across the 4 warps of a warpgroup, so each k16 step waits for the slowest warp's
       weight loads, where mma.sync warps run ahead independently;
     - X staging by a synchronous scatter (2 × LDG.128 → 8 × STS.32 per 32 B) instead of v2.2's cp.async.
   - The scatter is needed because v2's in-item k order is not wgmma's natural order.
3. **2 CTAs/SM starves the cold-expert stream.**
   - The only non-serialized instance (128 regs) halves the warps per SM.
   - On normal routings most units are cold, 1-pass experts, and they stream much slower: bs 256 471 vs 373 µs.
4. **What wins is the "few experts, many tokens" regime.**
   - The routings there: reference `dist="skewed"`, 2–5 touched experts, 32–128 tokens each.
   - Passes drop 2–4×, and the per-unit penalty is paid on few units.
   - On the judge's routings at bs 128/256 the hot experts (64 / ~120–143 tokens) are only part of the work. The
     ~20 cold experts carry the WG penalty.

The brief's literal design does not fix this; I did not build it. In that design, BF16 weights are dequantized once
into smem, and 8 token-group warps each read A through ldmatrix.
- Every token-group warp re-reads A from smem: 8 × 2 KB per 512 B item, about 3× over the smem budget at 3 TB/s.
- The sweep shows the same mechanism on the B side: mma.sync 32/64-token passes and TW 2 were already measured
  slower (k2a_v22.md, lab L5/L7).
- With A in smem, the WG path's register problem goes away only if A goes through smem once per warpgroup (wgmma SS
  form). That is item 2 of the next burst.

## Correctness tooling

- `tools/box/wgmma_unit.py` (box): one warpgroup, wgmma m64nNk16 RS against a CPU reference. Exact (max abs err 0)
  for N 8/16/32/64, in natural k order and in v2's item order with the X scatter.
  This pins the descriptor encoding (start >> 4, LBO 128 B, SBO 16·kw B, no swizzle, trans-b 0), the D fragment
  layout, and the sm_90a build through `load_inline`.
- `k2a_v23_emulate.py` (CPU, imports k2a_v23's repack): **ALL PASS** in 253 s, 13 cases including 4 WG ones:
  - bs 5 WG16 at KS 4/8 (atomics);
  - bs 33 WG32 at KS1 2 (SwiGLU(h) staging);
  - bs 128 skewed WG64 (experts 128 / 118 → 2 passes each);
  - bs 257 WG64 (155 / 127).
- What the emulator adds for WG:
  - It runs the WG `stage_x` formula literally into a flat smem image, with stale rows as NaN.
  - It reads B through the PTX canonical layout `((8,n),2):((1,SBO),LBO)` from the descriptor fields.
  - It checks against CuTe's SM90 layouts that the RS A 64×16 and 64×N C layouts equal the per-warp SM80 fragments,
    so `flush` is unchanged.
  - Negative control `K2A2_MUTATE=wgperm` (wrong core-matrix index in the scatter) FAILS (rel 1.4), as it should.
- A WG-only emulator run once reported bs 257 at 3.916e-3 (worst row 1.136e-2). Neither the full run nor two
  standalone reruns reproduced it: all gave 3.750e-3 / 3.948e-3, identical to the mma.sync path. The GPU checks passed
  on every plan at every case.
- compute-sanitizer: see below.

## Next GPU burst (code to prepare and emulate locally first; expected gains are estimates, haircut per R7)

1. **Mixed kernel at 4 CTAs/SM.** Experts with cnt > 16 take serialized WG32 units, cold experts take mma16 units,
   all in one unit list.
   - Expected: bs 256 −3..−8%, bs 128 −1..−3%.
   - The estimate comes from bs256 s657: hot experts take 17 of ~34 passes at 16 tokens per pass, 9 at 32.
   - Risk: the cold path's spills grow from the added code. The K2A2_HOT precedent raised 24 B to 96 B on v0. Check
     ptxas for the v1-equivalent before timing.
2. **Non-serialized WG at 3–4 CTAs/SM.**
   - How: A through smem in the wgmma SS form (no A registers across the async op), or a single A half with explicit
     `wait_group 0`.
   - Upper bound on hot-heavy routings: the serialization gap, 231.6 vs 290.7 µs (−20%). Unknown on normal routings.
3. **Weight prefetch for WG** (next group issued before consuming the current one, PF=1 style), against the
   warpgroup lockstep. Only fits in registers together with item 2.
4. **Natural-order repack for WG**, so X stages via cp.async and the synchronous scatter goes away.
   - Costs a separate weight copy (~0.9 GB) and 4× the scale bytes per lane.
   - Expected: −3..−5% of the WG unit cost; v2.2's cp.async change was worth 4% at bs 128/256.
5. **Validate `V2_PLAN_SKEWED` on the real decode_layer router at bs 32 (the 25–48 bucket) in situ.** It is −13% /
   −6% vs v2.2's best plan on reference-skewed routings.

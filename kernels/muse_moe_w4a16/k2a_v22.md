## What changed and what each change bought (same inputs, raw µs)

| change | effect |
|---|---|
| post-expert norm latency-batched: (token, column-quarter) items, slot pairs per load batch | bs 1 trace: norm 11.5 → ~6 µs; bs 16 133 → 120 (with the plan change) |
| 1-launch plan with the first W2 weight group issued before the W13→W2 grid barrier | bs 1 55.1 → 53.6 |
| cp.async X staging for ≥ 16-token variants (all pieces in flight, one wait) | bs 128 337.7 → 323.2, bs 256 385 → 369 |
| warp-parallel phase setup; trace marks (probe bit 4, globaltimer per CTA) | small; enables the box timeline |
| measured per-bucket plan | bs 16: 16-token variant at kst 2 → 8-token variant at kst 1 (133 → 120) |

Tried and rejected; all measured on the box, and the code is kept in `kernels/dev/` or compiled out:

| experiment | result |
|---|---|
| Double-buffered (unit, slice) step pipeline: cp.async next-step X, weights prefetched across units, decoded-unit ring | slower everywhere (bs 1 64 vs 57, bs 256 451 vs 379). Not spills: a 24 B spill variant was also slower; per-step overhead grew |
| Quantization-aware split-K, minimising ceil(U·KS/G)·(1/KS + α) | no gain. The CTA tails come from unit-time variance, not unit counts |
| More loads in flight per warp (U=8, or prefetch at U=4, at 3 CTAs/SM) | slower at every shape. This confirms stream_lab D's 4 CTAs × U=4 issue→consume optimum |
| Separate hot-expert launch, 64 tokens per warp-pass (`K2A2_HOT`) | slower: 425 vs 373 µs on the 143-token-expert routing, 345 vs 323 at bs 128. The 2-CTA/SM, 64-token instance is smem/MMA-bound and its extra launch and barriers cost more than the passes it saves |

## What limits it (box evidence)

- **Small shapes (bs 8–32) run at about lab D's compute-attached ceiling.**
  - bs 32: 594 MB of touched weights in ~203 µs is about 2.9 TB/s, against lab D's 3.02.
  - The per-CTA traces show 15–35% tail spread between median and slowest CTA in each phase.
  - The stream-only probe (no dequant/MMA) already reaches only 2.6–3.3 TB/s at these shapes.
  - Without performance counters (ncu is blocked on RunPod), the remaining gap cannot be attributed further.
- **bs 128/256 are dominated by hot experts in the judge's routings.**
  - The largest expert averages 64 / 120 tokens.
  - At 16 tokens/pass each extra pass re-dequantizes 28 MB.
  - Holding all tokens per weight sweep needs T/2 accumulator registers per thread at 16 rows per warp. The register
    budget forbids it in the streaming kernel, and the separate big-tile launch I built did not pay off.
  - A real weight-stationary path needs large CTA tiles: 256–512 rows × 64–128 tokens with smem-staged dequantized
    weights, i.e. a wgmma kernel with ~128 accumulator registers. That is a separate project.

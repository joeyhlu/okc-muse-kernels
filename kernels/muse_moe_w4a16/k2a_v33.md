## Design (bs 128/256 buckets only; bs 1–32 run v3.1's variant unchanged)
- Warm expert = more than 16 tokens. Its items are 64-token passes; wgmma m64nNk16 with N = 16/32/48/64 per pass
  (rounded up from the pass's tokens), A = v3.1's dequantized fragments in registers, B = the stage's X slice in
  core-matrix layout. P0 (normal launch) packs warm experts' x rows into that layout; K1 is now a PDL secondary whose
  warm CTAs take griddepcontrol.wait lazily (only before reusing a slot whose X is deferred). K1 writes warm
  intermediates straight into the W2 B layout; K2 reads them with one bulk copy per stage.
- **CTA role split inside each launch**: the first Gh CTAs take the warm stages, the rest the cold stages, each with
  its own stream-K range. Gh from a byte model of the work (36 half-KB of codes + scales per stage + 1 half-KB per
  staged token row). Cold-role CTAs re-cut the same 100 KB ring into 3 smaller stages.
- Why the split: any form of per-item branching between the wgmma and mma.sync paths in one loop made ptxas serialize
  every wgmma (C7520 "WG.AR in divergent path", then C7513 "non-wgmma instructions defining input registers"); a
  non-inlined wgmma function gives C7510 (pipeline crossing a call). The working form is the per-kb double-buffered
  loop (wait_group 1 before a 16-register A buffer is reused, wait_group 0 at the end of each stage) in a role-uniform
  code path. SASS/ptxas: no C75xx; 96 regs at 2 CTAs/SM with 144–208 B spill.

## Lab numbers (raw µs, `box_out/k33_lab2.log`, sweeps `k33_sweep1-6.log`)
- 8 experts × 64 / 128 tokens, full call: v3.1 298.8 / 577.8 → v3.3 200.7 / 314.9 (1 CTA/SM variants 176 / 277).
- 1 CTA/SM for the merged kernel (≤ 168 regs, no spill): −10 to −20% on judge-like routings (the cold majority
  starves); 2 CTAs/SM is the shipped variant.
- Role-split weight is sharp: warm-work scale 1.0 (byte model) best; ±25% costs 5–10% at bs256.
- Threshold 16 vs 24 vs 32: bs256 prefers 16 (1.109 vs 1.083 vs 1.070, 6 seeds); bs128 is within ±1.5%.

## Not done / next
- bs128 margin is thin: the role split is a static prediction; a dynamic tail (idle CTAs of one role stealing the
  other's last stages) would remove the imbalance that makes the weight so sensitive.
- Spills (144–208 B) in the merged kernel; a setmaxnreg role split (384-thread CTA) was not needed for correctness or
  serialization, untested for speed.

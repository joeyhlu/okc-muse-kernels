## What changed (kept)
1. **Post-norm sums of squares moved into K2**: whole W2 items reduce z = bf16(y)·(1+w) squares per route in the flush
   (shuffle + one red per warp); split W2 items go to a K2 epilogue warp (same pattern as K1: counter, last contributor
   reads back its 128 columns). K3 is now one pass (meta/weights/ss batch, then y batch, quarter items only).
   K2 has 10 warps (320 threads). Measured alone: ~−1.5 µs per call.
2. **Ring 3 stages instead of 4 for NC1** (smem 75 KB/CTA): −1 to −3 µs at bs 1–32 (box labs: NST 2–4 and 1–3
   CTAs/SM all within ±3%; deeper is slightly worse).
3. **y / ss zeroing moved to the consumer warps' idle time** before their first stage (off the producer's path).
4. **New code bit layout (BITPOS3)**: deq4 = {w&M, rotl(w,6)&M, rotl(w,12)&M, ((w>>6)&0x01000100)|((w<<2)&0x80C080C0)};
   bit-exact vs v2.2's decode (CPU check, all self-tests). ~25% fewer integer ops per word; measured gain ~0–1%.
5. Plan: NC1 for bs 1–32, NC2 for bs 128/256.

## Diagnosis of the remaining gap
- K1 fit over two sizes (math off, no X): steady ~4.0 TB/s + ~4.4 µs fixed (ramp + tail) per K1. With decode+mma:
  ~3.56 TB/s + ~3.8 µs.
- The ~11% math tax is NOT: integer op count (new decode didn't move it), X smem reads (B-from-registers probe was no
  faster), ring cycle (early release no faster), consumer warp count (3 CTAs/SM, 1 CTA/SM equal at bs8). Decode alone
  (no mma, no X) costs as much as decode+mma. Consistent with a per-SM ingest limit that scales with SM clock
  (1.98 → ~1.75 GHz under math = −11%), not verified (no clock trace taken).
- At bs128/256 the consumer is the limit on re-passes of hot experts (mma.sync per 8-token chunk); NC4/NC8 don't fix it
  — needs the wgmma hot-expert path (registers ≥ 128 → its own launch).
